Case key — what strong answers look like

Not a single right answer; a map of the terrain. Reward teams that find the structure, punish teams that ignore it.

Signal Sort answer key (M1). The six decision-critical statements are #1 (capital is a hard ceiling), #3 (one large line or two small — never three), #5 (brand attribute asymmetry: 84/79/81 vs modern 22 / personal 9), #7 (network repurposing degrades the agri base), #9 (talent gap + 15-hire cap), #11 (one shot in 72 hours → sequencing & reversibility). The other six are noise. 6/6 with a problem statement built on them is top-band M1.
The hidden structure (M2). The doors collide over shared assets — strong teams discover this and design around it:
  • Rajasthan (dry) is wanted by D1 (putty/adhesives) and D2 (nutrition powders).
  • Maharashtra (wet) is wanted by D3 (creams/serums) and D4 (paints/primers).
  • Capacity rule (one large or two small lines) makes most three-door plans physically impossible.
  • D5 consumes no factory at all — it monetises the network itself, but burns capital fastest and risks channel conflict.
  • D1 + D4 form a natural "construction stack": shared raw-material advantage, shared building-materials retail, same brand logic, and the mason (D4) becomes the pull engine for D1 products before Asian Paints/Berger arrive.
Strong strategy archetypes (M2/M5).
  • The Construction Stack: D4 first (cheapest, builds the mason channel), folding into D1 before the 18-month competitor entry. Capital fits even post-cut (₹140–170 + ₹180–220 stretches ₹420 — expect phasing or a scope cut, which is good thinking, not weakness).
  • The Patient Categorist: D1 as the cash engine + D2 as the slow category bet, both on staged gates (the two-small-lines configuration: D1 in Rajasthan at reduced scale + D2 sachets). Must acknowledge the shared-plant tension explicitly.
  • The Buy-not-Build pivot (post-twist): Saanjh at ₹85 Cr ≈ 1.37× revenue with 41% GM is a capital-efficient answer to Door 3's "build everything from zero" problem — it buys brand, talent and channel at a fraction of ₹280–320 Cr. Top teams will note integration/founder risk and that Bharat must stay an invisible holding brand.
  • The Platform Layer: D5 asset-light (₹140–160 Cr) layered on top of one physical door, framed as optionality. Reward teams that catch the asset-light variant in the CFO pack rather than pricing the full build.
Red flags (any metric).
  • Opening 3+ doors — capacity and capital make it fiction.
  • Ignoring the factory collision — e.g., recommending D1 + D2 at full scale with no mention of Rajasthan.
  • Spending the full ceiling post-twist — allocating ₹420 of ₹420 with no reserve tells the Group CFO they learned nothing.
  • A cosmetic twist response — shrinking every line item 16% instead of re-deciding.
  • No kill criteria — "one shot" demands knowing when to stop.
The twist, read correctly (M5). The Red Envelope tests whether the team's logic was load-bearing. The cut (₹500→₹420) should force re-sequencing or scope discipline, not panic. Saanjh is a genuine fork: engaging is smart if the team had aspiration-market logic; declining is smart if they argue focus and integration risk. Score the reasoning, not the choice.
